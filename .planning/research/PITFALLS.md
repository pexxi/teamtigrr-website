# Landing Page Pitfalls

**Domain:** SaaS landing pages for junior sports duty management
**Researched:** 2026-02-02
**Overall confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH (verified across multiple authoritative sources)

---

## Critical Pitfalls

Mistakes that cause significant conversion loss or require major rework.

### Pitfall 1: Vague or Self-Centered Headlines

**What goes wrong:** Headlines focus on the company/product ("Welcome to TeamTigrr") instead of visitor outcomes. Generic phrases like "Revolutionizing team management" could apply to any competitor.

**Why it happens:** Teams write from their own perspective rather than the visitor's. They describe *what* they built instead of *why* it matters.

**Consequences:**
- Visitors leave within 5 seconds unable to understand the value
- Conversion rates plummet (average SaaS landing pages convert at only 2-3%)
- Ad spend wasted on traffic that bounces

**Warning signs:**
- Headline could be swapped onto a competitor's page without anyone noticing
- Headline mentions company name but not visitor benefit
- Uses jargon or buzzwords ("AI-powered", "synergy", "revolutionary")

**Prevention:**
- Use outcome-driven headlines: "[Benefit] for [audience]" or "How [audience] [achieves outcome]"
- Test with 5-second rule: Can strangers identify what you offer and why it matters?
- Write headlines that complete: "I want to [headline]" from the visitor's perspective

**Phase to address:** Copy/Messaging phase (before visual design)

---

### Pitfall 2: Missing or Buried Privacy/Trust Messaging

**What goes wrong:** Key differentiators like privacy protection are not mentioned prominently, or trust signals are missing entirely.

**Why it happens:** Teams assume visitors will discover benefits through exploration. Privacy feels "boring" compared to features.

**Consequences:**
- 61% of visitors abandon transactions when security badges are absent
- Key differentiator becomes invisible competitive advantage
- Visitors default to competitors who demonstrate trustworthiness

**Warning signs:**
- Privacy/security benefits only mentioned in footer or separate page
- No trust signals (badges, guarantees, testimonials) near signup forms
- Bold claims without supporting evidence

**Prevention:**
- Place trust signals above-the-fold and near conversion points (forms, CTAs)
- Make privacy a headline-level benefit, not fine print
- Add social proof near every decision point

**Phase to address:** Content hierarchy phase and visual design phase

**TeamTigrr-specific:** Current page buries "Private & Secure" in feature cards below fold. Privacy is a key differentiator that should appear in hero section.

---

### Pitfall 3: Problem-Focused Negative Framing

**What goes wrong:** Landing page dwells on pain points and problems ("Retire the Spreadsheet & Chat Chaos", "The Old Chaos") instead of positive outcomes.

**Why it happens:** Problem-agitation-solution is a classic copywriting formula. Teams over-apply the "agitation" step.

**Consequences:**
- Messaging feels "tacky" and off-putting to visitors
- Negative emotions don't inspire action for voluntary products
- Parents/volunteers feel blamed rather than helped

**Warning signs:**
- Prominent "ban" icons or negative imagery
- Section headers use words like "chaos", "problem", "pain"
- More text devoted to current suffering than future benefits

**Prevention:**
- Lead with benefits and transformation, not problems
- Use "From X to Y" framing: positive destination emphasized
- Show what's possible, not just what's broken

**Phase to address:** Copy/Messaging phase

**TeamTigrr-specific:** Current "The Problem" section with WhatsApp chaos and "ban" overlay is exactly this pitfall. Reframe to benefits.

---

### Pitfall 4: Mobile Experience Afterthought

**What goes wrong:** Landing page designed for desktop first, mobile experience degraded or broken.

**Why it happens:** Designers/developers work on large screens. Mobile testing happens late if at all.

**Consequences:**
- Over 50% of traffic sees broken experience
- Conversion rates drop 40%+ on mobile vs desktop
- 80%+ of B2B buyers research on phones

**Warning signs:**
- CTA buttons tiny or hard to tap on mobile
- Text requires zooming to read
- Forms difficult to complete on phone
- No mobile testing before launch

**Prevention:**
- Design mobile-first, then scale up to desktop
- Test on real devices (not just browser resize)
- Ensure CTAs are thumb-friendly (minimum 44x44px tap targets)
- Check form field usability on mobile keyboards

**Phase to address:** Design phase and QA phase

---

### Pitfall 5: Multiple CTAs Creating Choice Paralysis

**What goes wrong:** Page presents too many options (Get Early Access, See How It Works, Learn More, Contact Us) and visitor takes none.

**Why it happens:** Teams want to accommodate every visitor type. Fear of missing conversions from different personas.

**Consequences:**
- Analysis paralysis leads to zero action
- Visitors confused about primary desired action
- Conversion tracking becomes unclear

**Warning signs:**
- More than one primary CTA style above the fold
- Different CTAs compete visually for attention
- No clear hierarchy between primary and secondary actions

**Prevention:**
- One landing page, one primary CTA (exceptions exist but should be intentional)
- Secondary CTAs should be visually subordinate
- Every element should support the primary conversion goal

**Phase to address:** Content hierarchy phase and design phase

## Moderate Pitfalls

Mistakes that cause conversion loss but are recoverable.

### Pitfall 6: Generic Social Proof

**What goes wrong:** Testimonials are vague ("Great product!"), anonymous, or use stock photos. Proof doesn't match target audience.

**Prevention:**
- Use specific outcomes with metrics when possible
- Include full names, companies, real photos
- Match testimonials to visitor personas (team leaders see team leader testimonials)
- Place testimonials strategically throughout page, not just in a carousel

**Phase to address:** Content gathering phase (before copy)

**TeamTigrr-specific:** Current page has no testimonials. For beta launch, even "Joining the beta from [Club Name]" with real club logos adds credibility.

---

### Pitfall 7: Slow Page Load Time

**What goes wrong:** Heavy images, external scripts, or unoptimized assets cause slow loading.

**Prevention:**
- Target under 3-second load time
- Optimize images (WebP, lazy loading)
- Minimize external script dependencies
- Test with Lighthouse or PageSpeed Insights

**Warning signs:**
- Bounce probability rises 123% as load time goes from 1s to 10s
- Users on mobile connections experience worst performance

**Phase to address:** Development phase and QA phase

---

### Pitfall 8: Form Friction Overload

**What goes wrong:** Signup forms ask for too much information (phone, company size, role) creating abandonment.

**Prevention:**
- Collect only essential fields (email is often enough for beta)
- Progressive profiling: collect more info over time
- Multi-step forms can feel lighter than one long form

**Warning signs:**
- Required fields that aren't strictly necessary
- Form abandonment rate above 50%

**Phase to address:** Form design phase

**TeamTigrr-specific:** Current form has 3 fields (email, sport, role). Consider if role is necessary for beta waitlist.

---

### Pitfall 9: Message Mismatch with Traffic Source

**What goes wrong:** Ad copy promises one thing, landing page delivers different messaging.

**Prevention:**
- Landing page headline should echo ad copy
- Semantic alignment: visitor expectations match what they see
- Create dedicated landing pages for different traffic sources/campaigns

**Warning signs:**
- High bounce rate from paid traffic
- Visitors from ads convert worse than organic

**Phase to address:** Campaign planning phase (when running ads)

---

### Pitfall 10: Feature-Focused Instead of Benefit-Focused

**What goes wrong:** Copy describes what product does (features) instead of what visitor gets (benefits).

**Prevention:**
- For every feature, ask "So what?" until you reach the benefit
- "Automated reminders" (feature) vs "Never chase a parent again" (benefit)
- Use "You get..." and "You can..." language

**Warning signs:**
- Copy reads like a spec sheet
- Technical terms without explanation
- No emotional resonance

**Phase to address:** Copy/Messaging phase

---

## Minor Pitfalls

Mistakes that cause friction but are easily fixed.

### Pitfall 11: Missing Urgency or Scarcity

**What goes wrong:** No reason for visitor to act now vs later.

**Prevention:**
- Beta messaging provides natural scarcity ("limited spots")
- Time-based offers when appropriate
- Avoid fake urgency which damages trust

**Phase to address:** Copy refinement phase

---

### Pitfall 12: Inconsistent Visual Hierarchy

**What goes wrong:** Most important elements aren't most visually prominent.

**Prevention:**
- Headline should be largest text
- Primary CTA should be highest-contrast element
- Trust signals visible but not competing with CTA

**Phase to address:** Design phase

---

### Pitfall 13: Grammar/Spelling Errors

**What goes wrong:** Small errors undermine professional credibility.

**Prevention:**
- Proofread by multiple people
- Use grammar checking tools
- Have non-team member review

**Phase to address:** QA phase

---

## Phase-Specific Warnings

| Phase | Likely Pitfall | Mitigation |
|-------|---------------|------------|
| Copy/Messaging | Vague headlines, negative framing, feature-focus | Write benefits-first, test with 5-second rule |
| Content Hierarchy | Privacy buried, multiple CTAs | Prioritize differentiators, single primary CTA |
| Social Proof | Missing or generic testimonials | Gather real quotes early, even beta users |
| Design | Mobile afterthought, slow load | Mobile-first design, performance budget |
| Form Design | Too many fields | Minimum viable data collection |
| QA | Grammar errors, broken mobile | Multi-device testing, external review |
