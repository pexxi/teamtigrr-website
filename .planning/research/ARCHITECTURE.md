# Landing Page Architecture

**Domain:** SaaS Landing Page for Junior Sports Duty Management
**Researched:** 2026-02-02
**Confidence:** HIGH (verified with multiple 2026 sources)

## Recommended Section Flow

High-converting SaaS landing pages follow a psychological persuasion sequence. Each section answers a visitor objection or advances them toward conversion.

### Section Order (with Rationale)

| Order | Section | Purpose | Why This Position |
|-------|---------|---------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Hero** | Hook with value proposition | First impression; must instantly communicate "what's in it for me" |
| 2 | **Social Proof (Lightweight)** | Build initial trust | Reduces skepticism before asking visitors to invest time reading |
| 3 | **Problem** | Validate their pain | Creates "yes, that's me" recognition before introducing solution |
| 4 | **Solution/How It Works** | Show the fix | Only after problem resonates; otherwise features feel abstract |
| 5 | **Features/Benefits** | Detail the value | Specific capabilities tied to benefits, not just feature lists |
| 6 | **Use Cases / Demo** | Make it tangible | Shows product in action for their specific context |
| 7 | **Social Proof (Deep)** | Overcome remaining doubt | Testimonials from people like them; quantified results |
| 8 | **FAQ / Objection Handling** | Remove final friction | Addresses "what if" concerns that block conversion |
| 9 | **Final CTA** | Convert | After all objections addressed; clear single action |
| 10 | **Footer** | Secondary navigation | Legal, contact, social links |

### Why Problem Comes Before Solution

Research consistently shows that describing the problem BEFORE the solution dramatically improves conversion. Visitors who don't yet feel understood will dismiss feature explanations as "not for me." The sequence is:

1. "We understand your pain" (Problem)
2. "Here's how we fix it" (Solution)
3. "Here's proof it works" (Social Proof)

## Information Hierarchy

### What Gets Emphasis (Priority Order)

1. **Hero Headline** - Single most important element. Must communicate transformation, not features.
   - Current: "Every Game Covered. Every Duty Handled." - Good outcome focus

2. **Primary CTA** - Must be visible without scrolling, repeated 2-3x throughout page
   - Current: "Get Early Access" - Good urgency language

3. **Problem Visualization** - Makes abstract pain concrete
   - Current: Excel/WhatsApp mockup - Excellent execution

4. **Benefit Statements** - Features translated to user outcomes
   - Current: "Fair Assignments," "Automated Nudges," "Mobile First" - Good

5. **Social Proof** - Most underutilized on current page

### Above-the-Fold Requirements

Everything above the fold (before scrolling) must include:
- Clear headline communicating the transformation
- Subheadline explaining how
- Visual showing the product/outcome
- Primary CTA button
- Optional: lightweight social proof (logos, "X teams trust us")

Current page nails 4/5 of these. Missing lightweight social proof.

### Visual Hierarchy Principles

| Element | Treatment |
|---------|-----------|
| Headlines | Large, bold, benefit-focused |
| Body copy | Scannable; short paragraphs |
| CTAs | High contrast color (orange on current page) |
| Social proof | Real faces/logos increase trust |
| Features | Icons + short text; grid layout works well |

## Recommended Build Order

Based on impact-to-effort ratio and conversion impact:

### Phase 1: Quick Wins (High Impact, Low Effort)

1. **Add lightweight social proof after hero**
   - "Trusted by X teams" or placeholder for beta testimonials
   - Can be simple text initially; logos later

2. **Add FAQ section before final CTA**
   - Addresses common objections
   - Good for SEO/AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)
   - 5-7 questions covering: pricing, setup time, privacy, sport support

3. **Strengthen hero subheadline**
   - Current subheadline is good but could emphasize team leader benefit more
   - Consider: "The duty management app that keeps your game days running smooth"

### Phase 2: Medium Effort, High Impact

4. **Add "How It Works" section**
   - 3-step visual flow: Setup -> Assign -> Relax
   - Reduces "is this complicated?" objection
   - Place between Problem and Features sections

5. **Add testimonial section**
   - Even 1-2 beta tester quotes help
   - Format: Quote + Name + Role + Team type
   - Place after Features, before CTA

### Phase 3: Polish & Optimization

6. **Refine copy throughout**
   - Move from feature-focused to outcome-focused language
   - Every feature should answer "so what?"

7. **Add secondary CTA option**
   - Lower commitment: "See a demo" or "Watch video"
   - Captures visitors not ready for signup

8. **Mobile optimization pass**
   - Current page is responsive but verify touch targets, CTA visibility

## Patterns to Follow

### Pattern 1: Feature-Benefit Pairs

**What:** Never state a feature without its benefit
**When:** All feature descriptions

| Instead of | Write |
|------------|-------|
| "Automated reminders" | "Automated reminders so you never chase parents again" |
| "Swap system" | "One-tap swaps that keep duties covered without your involvement" |
| "Privacy controls" | "Contact details stay private, visible only to those who need them" |

### Pattern 2: Problem-Agitate-Solve (PAS)

**What:** Structure problem section to intensify pain before offering relief
**When:** Problem section

1. **Problem:** "Managing a junior team is hard enough"
2. **Agitate:** "Don't spend your evenings staring at spreadsheets, scrolling through endless WhatsApp threads"
3. **Solve:** "TeamTigrr ensures your game-day duties are always covered"

Current page does this well.

### Pattern 3: Social Proof Ladder

**What:** Progress from lightweight to heavyweight proof
**When:** Throughout page

1. **After Hero:** Numbers or logos ("50+ teams", company logos)
2. **Mid-page:** Short testimonials (1-2 sentences)
3. **Before CTA:** Full testimonials with photos, or case study snippets

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

### Anti-Pattern 1: Feature-First Messaging

**What:** Leading with features before establishing problem
**Why bad:** Visitors don't know why they should care
**Instead:** Always establish problem context first

### Anti-Pattern 2: Multiple CTAs Competing

**What:** "Sign up," "Watch demo," "Download guide," "Contact us" all equally prominent
**Why bad:** Choice paralysis reduces conversion
**Instead:** One primary CTA; secondary options visually subordinate

### Anti-Pattern 3: Generic Social Proof

**What:** "Trusted by thousands" without specifics
**Why bad:** Vague claims feel like marketing speak
**Instead:** Specific numbers, real names, recognizable contexts

### Anti-Pattern 4: Wall of Text

**What:** Long paragraphs explaining features
**Why bad:** Visitors scan, don't read
**Instead:** Short paragraphs, bullet points, visual breaks

## TeamTigrr-Specific Recommendations

### Primary Audience: Team Leaders

Frame messaging around team leader pain points:
- Time spent coordinating volunteers
- Last-minute scrambles when parents cancel
- Fairness concerns (same parents always volunteering)
- Privacy worries about sharing contact details

### Secondary Audience: Parents

Parents visit after hearing about app from team leader. They need:
- Quick understanding of what it does
- How it benefits them (not more work)
- Trust that their info is private

Consider: Small section or FAQ item addressing parents specifically.

### Tone Calibration

Current tone is sporty and energetic - maintain this. Key principles:
- Active voice ("TeamTigrr ensures" not "duties are ensured")
- Benefit-focused ("so you can" not "we provide")
- Conversational but professional (no corporate jargon)
